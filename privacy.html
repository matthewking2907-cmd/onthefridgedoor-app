Privacy

Last updated 27 November 2026

The short version. Your family’s plans live on your own devices. If you switch on sync, a copy is kept on our server in London so your devices can share it, and nobody outside your household can see it. We don’t show adverts, we don’t track you around the web, and we never sell or share your data. We only count totals, like how many households use the meal plan, never what’s in anyone’s plan. If you use the AI helpers, what you give them goes to Anthropic, only to write the answer, and only after you’ve agreed.

Who we are

On the FridgeDoor is a family planner run by Matthew King in the United Kingdom. For anything about your data, email hello@onthefridgedoor.com.

What stays on your device

Everything you put in the app (your family’s names, children’s birth month and year, meal plans, the recipes you add or change, and a record of the dinners you’ve had, shopping lists, chores and the rewards children collect (with any reason you give for extra ones, like “Ate up their dinner”), each child’s pet (the name they give it, how far it has grown, and the sleep and wake times you set), events, tasks, birthdays and gift ideas, the renewal dates you add to Paperwork, routines and habits with each child’s progress, school holidays and kit days, and any food needs and allergies you add) is saved in the app on your phone, tablet or computer. Without sync, it never leaves that device.

Some settings always stay on each device and never go to our server: the parent PIN, the colour theme, how you’ve arranged Today and the kitchen display, and copies of connected calendars. If reminders are on, the titles and times of connected-calendar events in the next two weeks (just “Busy” for a busy-only calendar) go into the reminder schedule on our server, so the reminders can be sent.

Children

We ask for each child’s name and birth month and year only, never the full date, so the app can suggest chores and essentials that suit their age. Children don’t have accounts or give an email address, and we don’t collect anything from them directly. A parent can connect a child’s phone or tablet with a one-time link: the device gets an anonymous sign-in tied to the household, labelled with the child’s name so parents can see and remove it. A parent can lock the app on a shared device so children can’t change settings or see gift ideas. A child’s pet grows from the rewards already in the app; it has no chat, no shop for real money and no adverts, and it never sends notifications.

Food needs and allergies

You can add food needs for each person: any of the 14 allergens UK law lists (and whether an allergy is severe), coeliac disease, vegetarian or vegan, and foods to avoid. This is health information, so the app asks first and saves nothing until you agree. The app uses it only to mark meals that don’t suit someone, in the meal chooser and recipes.

You choose where it’s kept. Share with our household syncs it like the rest of your plan, so everyone in your household sees the warnings. Keep on this device only stores it on that phone or tablet and never sends it to our server. You can switch between the two, or use Delete all food needs, in Settings › Your household.

The app reads ingredient names, not packets, and products change, so it never says a meal is safe: always check the label.

AI helpers

Three optional helpers use AI: Quick add (type, say or snap a photo of a note, letter or email, and it sorts it into plans, tasks, shopping and dates), Adapt a recipe (for a slow cooker, vegetarian, milder and so on) and Bring in a recipe (describe a dish, or paste a recipe or a link). They’re being tried out and are only switched on for some households. Each grown-up is asked once, in the app, before anything is sent, and can withdraw in Settings › Features.

When you tap the button, and only then, we send what’s needed to Anthropic, which makes the Claude AI, so it can write the answer:

  • Quick add: what you type or say, any photo or screenshot you add, your family’s first names (and who is a child), and today’s date and time.
  • Adapt a recipe: the recipe and what you ask for.
  • Bring in a recipe: what you describe or paste, and the names of the ingredients your household already uses (so the shopping list adds them up). If you give a link, our server opens that page to read the recipe; the website sees a visit from our server, not from you.

Anthropic uses it only to answer us. It doesn’t use it to train its AI, its staff don’t read it unless its automated safety checks flag it, and it deletes it within 30 days (or up to 2 years if it has been flagged). Anthropic is in the USA and handles it under its data processing terms, which include the UK’s approved safeguards for sending data abroad. We don’t keep your words or pictures: our server passes them on and keeps only the line described in the table above.

A photo of a school letter or email may mention your children. Only what comes out of it, such as dates, kit and things to do, is added to your plans, and only once you’ve checked the list. Speak uses your phone’s own speech-to-text (Google’s or Apple’s, under their privacy policies): we only ever get the words. AI can get things wrong, so nothing is saved until you’ve looked it over, and it never says a recipe is safe for an allergy: always check the label.

Companies that help run the app

We use a small number of services, and only for what’s described here:

  • Supabase runs our database, sign-in and reminder server, on servers in London, UK.
  • Resend sends the sign-in code emails from Ireland (EU), so it receives the email address. It also delivers feedback you send from the app to our inbox.
  • GitHub hosts the app’s files (like any web host, it sees the internet address of devices that load the app) and keeps our weekly backups, encrypted so only we can open them, for 90 days.
  • Cloudflare runs our domain’s address book (DNS).
  • Google Play, if you install the Android app from it: Google handles the download and updates under its own privacy policy. The Android app is the same app as the website and sends us nothing extra.
  • Notification services from Google, Apple, Microsoft or Mozilla deliver reminders to your device. They carry the reminder’s text, as with any app notification.
  • Google, Microsoft or Apple, if you connect one of their calendars: our server fetches the calendar from the link you give it.
  • Anthropic (USA), if you use the AI helpers: it receives what you give them, as described in AI helpers, deletes it within 30 days and doesn’t train on it.

The app doesn’t load fonts, adverts or trackers from anyone else.

Why we can use your data (UK GDPR)

We use your data to provide the service you’ve asked for: our lawful basis is contract for parents who sign in, and legitimate interests for the family details parents choose to add, which are only used to make the app work for that family. For food needs and allergies, which are health information, it’s your explicit consent, given in the app before anything is saved; you can withdraw it at any time by deleting them. For the AI helpers it’s your consent, given in the app before anything is sent; you can withdraw it at any time in Settings › Features.

Your choices and rights

  • See or take a copy: Settings › Backup › Export backup saves everything the app holds for your household as a file.
  • Correct: change anything in the app at any time.
  • Delete: Settings › Sync across devices › More › Delete my account removes your sign-in, your device’s reminders, your calendar links and your AI helpers agreement and uses. If you’re the last parent in the household, all of its shared data goes too. Encrypted backups older than the deletion expire within 90 days. Start again in Settings clears a device.
  • Ask us: email hello@onthefridgedoor.com for anything else, including a copy of what we hold.
  • Complain: you can contact the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Feedback

If you send feedback from Settings while signed in, we keep your message, your sign-in email (so we can reply) and a few device details: app version, phone type and which features are on. It never includes names, meals or chores. We read it on our server and it’s emailed to our inbox. It’s deleted if you delete your account, or if you ask us. If you report an answer from the AI helpers, the answer and what you typed go with it (never a photo). If you’re not signed in, the app opens an email to us instead, or copies the message for you to send yourself.

Counting totals

To see whether the app is helping, we count totals on our server from what it already stores: how many households there are, how many were active this week, how many have more than one parent or a child’s device, and which features are switched on. Only the app’s owner can see these numbers. They are never about one household, and they never include names or what’s in anyone’s plan.

Changes

If we change how we use your data, we’ll update this page and say so in the app before it takes effect.
